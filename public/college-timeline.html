Education

College Timeline

Nobody applies to college in one weekend. Here is the shape of it at a glance, and the detail underneath when you need it.

Brief Overview

  1. 9th & 10th Do Well in Class Join Extracurriculars
  2. 11th Fall Take the PSAT
  3. 11th Spring Take the SAT or ACT
  4. 11th Spring Ask for Letters of Recommendation
  5. 12th Summer Build a College Application List Write Your Personal Statement
  6. 12th Fall Apply to Colleges File the FAFSA

Detailed Overview

9th Grade
  • Take classes that interest you and prioritize learning. Develop study habits that will carry you through difficult classes in the future.
  • Join one or two activities you actually care about. Continuity and consistency — colleges prefer four years in one thing more than one year in six things.

10th Grade

Fall

  • Take the PSAT if your school offers it to sophomores. It does not count for National Merit this year so treat it as free practice with no stakes.

Winter

  • Apply to College Horizons. Open to Native American, Alaska Native, and Native Hawaiian sophomores and juniors with a 3.0 core GPA. Five components plus a parent form.

Spring

  • Start paying attention to which teachers know your work best. Those could be your recommendation letters for college.

11th Grade

Fall

  • Take the PSAT/NMSQT. This is the one that counts for National Merit Scholarship consideration.
  • Decide whether you will take the SAT, the ACT, or both. Colleges accept either equally.

Winter

  • Register for a standardized test about 4–6 weeks ahead of when you want to take it.
  • Begin test prep through online materials. Take full-length timed practice tests, then review every question you missed.
  • Request a fee waiver from your counselor if cost is a barrier. Waivers cover the exams and, importantly, carry over to college application fees in the fall.
  • Apply to College Horizons as a junior if you did not go as a sophomore. This is your last year of eligibility.

Spring

  • Take the SAT or ACT for the first time. Taking it in spring leaves room to retake in summer or early fall, which is the whole reason to go early.
  • Ask for recommendation letters before the year ends. Two teachers from junior-year core subjects, ideally one STEM. Give them your activity list, and ask early.
  • Build a college list of 8–12 schools across reach, target, and safety.
  • Visit campuses if you can, virtually if you cannot. Most colleges run virtual tours and some have Native-specific admissions contacts.

Summer before senior year

  • Write your personal statement early.
  • Retake the SAT or ACT if your first score was below your target.
  • The Common App opens August 1. Create your account and start filling in the parts — activities, honors, coursework.
  • Draft answers to the essay prompts you can anticipate: why this school, what community means to you, a challenge you have faced.

12th Grade

September

  • Finalize your college list and record every deadline in one place.
  • Take your final SAT or ACT if you are retaking. October is the last sitting that reliably reaches Early Decision deadlines.
  • QuestBridge National College Match closes in late September. Full four-year scholarships at selective colleges for students from lower-income households. One free application.

October

  • File the FAFSA. It opens October 1 each year. File as early as you can.
  • File the CSS Profile if any of your colleges require it. Opens October 1, costs $25 for the first school, and is waived for lower-income families.
  • Submit Early Decision and Early Action applications, usually due November 1 or November 15.
  • The Gates Scholarship and Coca-Cola Scholars both close in the fall. Both are full-ride or near-full-ride awards for seniors.

November & December

  • Finish and submit regular decision applications. Most are due January 1.
  • Write supplemental essays. The “why us” essays require actual research on each school.
  • Apply to the Cobell Scholarship and your tribe's own programs. Cobell applications typically open in winter with a spring deadline; tribal programs vary, so ask your education department directly.
  • Ask your counselor to send transcripts and school reports.

January & February

  • Submit remaining applications and confirm every college received everything. Portals show what is missing.
  • Keep applying to scholarships. Smaller tribal and regional awards are far less competitive than national ones simply because fewer people apply.

March & April

  • Decisions arrive.
  • Appeal an aid offer if your family's circumstances changed or another school offered more. Colleges can adjust, and most students never ask.
  • Visit if you can, and try to speak with Native students currently enrolled.
  • Decide by May 1, the national deadline.

Summer after graduation

  • Complete housing, orientation, and placement testing.
  • Accept your aid package and complete loan counseling if you are borrowing.
  • Find the Native student center or organization before you arrive and introduce yourself. Nearly everyone featured in Stories & Voices names community as what carried them through.
