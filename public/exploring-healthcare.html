Section one

Exploring Healthcare

Healthcare is much larger than becoming a doctor. Below are twenty professions, each with the real path into it — what the schooling takes, how long, and what the work pays. Some take six years past high school. Some take two.

Time after high school

Dental Hygienist

2–3 years

Cleans teeth, screens for oral disease, takes X-rays, and teaches patients how to care for their mouths. Oral health is one of the largest untreated needs in Indian Country, and hygienists are often the first to catch a problem.

Degree
Associate degree in dental hygiene, then a state licensing exam
Length
About 3 years after high school
Median pay
$98,100

Respiratory Therapist

2 years

Treats people who cannot breathe well — asthma, COPD, premature infants, patients on ventilators. Respiratory therapists were on the front line of the pandemic and remain in short supply in rural and tribal hospitals.

Degree
Associate degree, though a bachelor's is increasingly preferred
Length
2–4 years after high school
Median pay
$82,280

Community Health Representative

Entry level

Connects people to care, follows up with patients at home, runs prevention programs, and translates between a clinic and the community it serves. The Community Health Representative program is tribally run and one of the most direct ways to serve your own nation. Many people start here and go further.

Degree
High school diploma plus on-the-job training or a certificate
Length
Under 1 year to start
Median pay
$51,850

Registered Nurse

4 years

The backbone of nearly every hospital and clinic. Nurses assess patients, give treatments, and spend more time at the bedside than anyone else. Only 0.4% of nurses in this country identify as Native American — a gap felt in every tribal clinic.

Degree
BSN (4 years) or ADN (2 years), then the NCLEX-RN exam
Length
2–4 years after high school
Median pay
$97,550

Social Worker

4–6 years

Works with families in crisis, in hospitals, schools, behavioral health, and child welfare. Indian Child Welfare Act cases, substance use, and intergenerational trauma all run through this profession, and 0.8% of social workers are Native.

Degree
BSW for entry roles; MSW (2 more years) for clinical licensure
Length
4–6 years after high school
Median pay
$61,780

Health Education Specialist

4 years

Designs and runs the programs that keep people from getting sick in the first place — diabetes prevention, tobacco cessation, youth wellness, vaccination drives. Often the most culturally grounded work in a tribal health department.

Degree
Bachelor's in health education or public health; CHES certification common
Length
4 years after high school
Median pay
$64,070

Clinical Laboratory Scientist

4 years

Runs the tests that diagnoses depend on — blood work, cultures, tissue samples. Quiet, technical, essential work, and a strong fit for students who love science but are unsure about patient-facing roles.

Degree
Bachelor's in medical laboratory science (associate for technician roles)
Length
2–4 years after high school
Median pay
$62,930

Dietitian / Nutritionist

5–6 years

Uses food to prevent and treat disease. Given the rates of diabetes and heart disease in AI/AN communities, dietitians who understand traditional foods and food sovereignty are urgently needed.

Degree
Bachelor's plus a master's (required for new RDs), plus supervised practice
Length
About 6 years after high school
Median pay
$77,130

Health Services Manager

4–6 years

Runs clinics, departments, and tribal health programs — budgets, staffing, contracts, and strategy. Self-governance means tribes increasingly run their own health systems, and they need their own people managing them.

Degree
Bachelor's; a master's (MHA, MPH, MBA) is common for senior roles
Length
4–6 years after high school
Median pay
$123,860

Physician (MD / DO)

11+ years

Diagnoses and treats illness, and in tribal communities often serves as surgeon, emergency physician, and primary care provider at once. Just 0.3% of attending physicians are AI/AN. The path is long, and thousands of Native people have walked it.

Degree
Bachelor's → MCAT → 4 years medical school → 3–7 years residency
Length
11–15 years after high school
Median pay
$275,930 (varies widely by specialty)

Nurse Practitioner

6–8 years

Diagnoses, treats, and prescribes much as a physician does, with a nursing foundation. In many rural and tribal clinics the NP is the primary provider. One of the fastest-growing professions in the country.

Degree
BSN → RN licensure → MSN or DNP
Length
6–8 years after high school
Median pay
$134,920

Physician Assistant

6–7 years

Examines patients, orders tests, prescribes, and assists in surgery, working with a physician. A shorter route than medical school to much of the same clinical work, and PAs can move between specialties.

Degree
Bachelor's plus a 2–3 year master's program, then the PANCE exam
Length
6–7 years after high school
Median pay
$135,880

Pharmacist

6–8 years

Dispenses and manages medications, counsels patients, and catches dangerous drug interactions. In small tribal communities the pharmacist is often the most accessible health professional a person sees all year.

Degree
2–4 years of prerequisites, then a 4-year PharmD
Length
6–8 years after high school
Median pay
$140,920

Dentist

8 years

Diagnoses and treats disease of the teeth, gums, and mouth. Untreated dental disease is one of the starkest health disparities in Indian Country, and IHS dental vacancies are persistent.

Degree
Bachelor's → DAT → 4 years of dental school (DDS or DMD)
Length
8 years after high school
Median pay
$191,350

Physical Therapist

7 years

Restores movement after injury, surgery, or stroke. Deeply hands-on work built on relationships with patients over weeks and months.

Degree
Bachelor's plus a 3-year Doctor of Physical Therapy (DPT)
Length
7 years after high school
Median pay
$105,280

Occupational Therapist

6–7 years

Helps people do the things their lives require — dressing, writing, working, playing — after injury, illness, or developmental difference. Heavy focus on children and elders.

Degree
Bachelor's plus a master's or doctorate in occupational therapy
Length
6–7 years after high school
Median pay
$101,280

Speech-Language Pathologist

6 years

Treats speech, language, and swallowing disorders in children and adults. SLPs who understand Native language revitalization and bilingual development are exceptionally rare and badly needed in tribal schools.

Degree
Bachelor's plus a master's in speech-language pathology, plus clinical fellowship
Length
About 6 years after high school
Median pay
$97,870

Psychologist

8–12 years

Provides therapy, assessment, and research on mental health. Historical trauma, suicide, and substance use are among the most pressing issues facing Native youth, and culturally grounded Native psychologists are scarce.

Degree
Bachelor's → PhD or PsyD → supervised hours → state licensure
Length
8–12 years after high school
Median pay
$99,110

Epidemiologist

6 years

Investigates why disease shows up where it does and what stops it. Tribal epidemiology centers use this work to make the case for funding, policy, and data sovereignty — the right of nations to control data about their own people.

Degree
Bachelor's plus a Master of Public Health or equivalent
Length
About 6 years after high school
Median pay
$87,220

Veterinarian

8 years

Cares for animals, and in rural and reservation settings often guards human health too — rabies, livestock disease, and food safety all sit with the vet. Reservation communities frequently have no veterinary access at all.

Degree
Bachelor's plus a 4-year Doctor of Veterinary Medicine (DVM)
Length
8 years after high school
Median pay
$130,100

Median annual wages are national figures from the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Outlook Handbook (May 2025). Actual pay varies considerably by state, setting, and specialty. The national median wage across all occupations was $50,980.

You do not have to pick one today

Almost everyone on the Stories & Voices page changed direction at least once. One started at a community college. One served nine years in the Army first. One was the first physician her tribe ever had. Begin with the science courses, find people who will vouch for you, and the path narrows on its own.
