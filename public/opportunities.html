Opportunities & Support

Scholarships and mentorship exist for Indigenous students in the health professions. This section gathers those opportunities in one place so our students can find them, take advantage of them, and pursue their goals.

Scholarships for Native students

Apply to several. These are not mutually exclusive, and students routinely stack them. Start with your own tribe, then work outward.

Indian Health Service Scholarship Program

There are three tiers of this scholarship. The Health Professions Preparatory Scholarship is for students still completing prerequisite or preparatory coursework before they can be admitted to a health professions program — it funds up to two years, and for 2026–2027 IHS lists only pre-nursing as eligible. The Pre-Graduate Scholarship is for students already enrolled in a bachelor's degree that feeds a professional program, such as pre-medicine or pre-dentistry, and funds up to four years. Neither carries a service commitment. The Health Professions Scholarship covers tuition and fees plus a monthly stipend for students already in a health professions program, in exchange for service at an IHS or tribal facility after graduation.

College juniors, seniors & professional students Tribal enrollment or descent required No service commitment (Prep & Pre-Grad)

Cobell Scholarship

Funds Native American students in undergraduate, graduate, vocational, and fellowship study. Created from the Cobell settlement, and one of the largest sources of need- and merit-based aid available to Native students. Undergraduate and graduate applications typically open in winter with a spring deadline; vocational applications run year-round.

All levels Vocational tracks too Confirm current deadline

Native Forward Scholars Fund

Formerly the American Indian Graduate Center, and the largest scholarship provider for Native students in the country. Administers many programs across undergraduate, graduate, and professional study, including awards specifically for health professions students. Their Scholarship Finder is the single best tool for discovering awards you did not know existed — start there before searching anywhere else.

Undergraduate & graduate Many awards, one place

American Indian College Fund — American Indian Scholars Program

Awards roughly 6,000 scholarships each year to American Indian and Alaska Native undergraduate and graduate students, including descendants. One application puts you in consideration for many awards, so the effort-to-opportunity ratio is excellent. Applications generally open in spring for the following academic year.

~6,000 awards per year One application, many awards

BIA Higher Education Grant & your tribe's education department

The least publicized and most overlooked money available. The Bureau of Indian Affairs funds higher education grants, and nearly every tribe runs its own higher education department with scholarships for its citizens. Call your tribe's education office directly — these awards are often less competitive than national ones simply because fewer people apply.

Often less competitive Contact your tribe directly Start here first

Chief Manuelito Scholarship — Navajo Nation

A merit scholarship of $7,000 per year for enrolled members of the Navajo Nation, administered by the Office of Navajo Nation Scholarship and Financial Assistance. Requires a Certificate of Indian Blood showing one-quarter or more Navajo blood quantum, and completion of Navajo Language and Navajo Government coursework before high school graduation — online options exist if your school does not offer them. Recipients maintain a 3.0 GPA and 12+ credit hours per term.

Navajo Nation citizens $7,000 per year Plan coursework in high school

Cherokee Nation Foundation Scholarships

Scholarships for Cherokee Nation citizens across many fields. The Nation has also recently committed substantial new funding specifically to health careers, including a Public Health and Wellness Merit Scholarship for students pursuing health services, public health, and behavioral health degrees who intend to work for the tribe after graduating. Worth checking both the Foundation and Cherokee Nation Education Services.

Cherokee Nation citizens New health-career funding

Hawai'i Community Foundation

Administers more than 300 scholarship funds, a number of them specifically benefiting Native Hawaiian students, covering both four-year degrees and career and technical education at community colleges. One application portal searches across the whole catalog.

Native Hawaiian students 300+ funds, one portal

Major national scholarships

These are not Native-specific, but they are among the largest awards in the country and Native students are strongly encouraged to apply. Most are for high school seniors and most are need-based, so a modest family income is an advantage here, not a barrier. Deadlines cluster in the fall of senior year — mark them early.

The Gates Scholarship

A full ride for 300 outstanding minority high school seniors from low-income households each year, covering the full cost of attendance that is not already met by other financial aid — tuition, fees, room, board, books, and transportation. One of the few awards that genuinely closes the entire gap.

High school seniors Full cost of attendance Applies in the fall

QuestBridge — National College Match

Matches high-achieving students from households generally under about $65,000 a year with full four-year scholarships at some of the country's most selective colleges, a package QuestBridge values at over $360,000. You apply once, free, and are considered for early admission plus the scholarship at your ranked partner colleges. The College Prep Scholars program does the same thing a year earlier, for juniors.

High school juniors & seniors Free to apply Match deadline is late September

Coca-Cola Scholars Program

A $20,000 award for 150 high school seniors chosen each year from more than 100,000 applicants. Open to seniors with at least a 3.0 GPA heading to an accredited U.S. institution. Highly competitive, but the application itself is short enough that it is worth the attempt.

High school seniors $20,000 Opens August, closes fall

Jack Kent Cooke Foundation College Scholarship

Up to $55,000 per year for as many as four years, plus advising and a support network that continues through college. For high school seniors with financial need, a minimum 3.5 GPA, and strong test scores. The ongoing advising is as valuable as the money for a first-generation student.

High school seniors Up to $55,000/year Advising included

Programs for high school students

If you are still in high school, these are the highest-leverage things you can do. Most are free or close to it.

College Horizons

A five-day crash course in college admissions and financial aid for Native high school sophomores and juniors, held each summer on a university campus. Students work directly with college counselors and admissions officers on essays, applications, and aid. Tuition, room, and board are all covered — the only cost to you is round-trip airfare. Open to enrolled citizens and descendants of American Indian tribes, Alaska Native tribes and villages, ANCSA shareholders, and Native Hawaiians, with a 3.0 minimum GPA in core courses.

Sophomores & juniors Tuition, room & board covered Applications close in winter

LEDA Scholars Program

A highly selective, fully funded leadership and college-admissions program for high-achieving students from low-income backgrounds, including a summer institute at Princeton and years of continuing support. Expects roughly a 3.7 unweighted GPA, top-10% class rank, and family income under 200% of the federal poverty guidelines. Begin preparing in sophomore year if this is your target.

High school juniors Fully funded Highly selective

Native Explorers Program

A hands-on science program run through Oklahoma State University's Center for Health Sciences in Tulsa, built around anatomy and vertebrate paleontology. Students work in the field and the lab, learning the scientific method alongside traditional ways of knowing. One of the most direct ways to find out whether you actually like doing science before you commit to a degree in it.

High school & college students Hands-on fieldwork Oklahoma

UNITY 25 Under 25 Leadership Award

National recognition for 25 Native youth aged 14 to 24 who are already leading in their communities. Recipients receive a handmade beaded medallion and eight months of leadership training from UNITY. A strong thing to have on a college or scholarship application, and a way into a national network of Native young people.

Ages 14–24 8 months of training Applications close in spring

Center for Native American Youth

A national advocacy and resource organization for Native youth, with a Youth Advisory Board, policy work, and a steady stream of opportunity listings. Good to follow even if you are not applying to anything specific yet — much of what reaches Native students first passes through here.

All Native youth Advocacy & opportunity listings

Pre-health pathway programs

Structured programs built specifically to get Native students into and through health professions school. Several are free, and several are explicitly designed for students whose applications need strengthening.

Summer Health Professions Education Program (SHPEP)

A free national summer program for college freshmen and sophomores interested in the health professions. Housing, meals, and a stipend are typically covered. If you are in your first two years of college and only do one thing from this page, apply to this — it is free, well regarded, and opens doors at the schools that host it.

College years 1–2 Free, with stipend Applications close early in the year

AIAHPP — American Indians Accessing Health Professions Program

A free AAIP program for American Indian and Alaska Native students at undergraduate, community, and tribal colleges. Covers different health careers, the admissions process for health professions schools, financial aid, and research opportunities, with problem-based learning and hands-on clinical simulations. Explicitly built for students who are not at a big research university and do not have pre-health advising on tap.

Undergraduate, community & tribal college Free Mentors included

Mayo Clinic Native American Pathway Program

A five-week virtual summer program offering coaching for medical school readiness, career exploration, an introduction to scientific research, and remote clinical observation. The curriculum is built on culturally resonant practices with attention to identity, resilience, and well-being. Virtual, so there is no relocation cost.

College students 5 weeks, virtual Research exposure

Wy'east Medicine Pathway

A tuition-free, 10-month post-baccalaureate program for American Indian and Alaska Native learners preparing for medical school, running mid-September through early June. Culturally rooted specifically for AI/AN students, it prepares you for both the academic content and the spaces you will be navigating in medical school. One of the strongest options for a student whose application needs strengthening.

Post-baccalaureate Tuition-free AI/AN specific

INMED — American Indians into Medicine

A federal grant program supporting universities to enroll and support AI/AN students in health careers. Provides tutoring, career counseling, help with scholarship and financial aid applications, summer educational sessions, and travel grants to health conferences. Current grantee institutions include the University of Alaska Anchorage, the University of New Mexico, the University of North Dakota, and the University of Wisconsin.

Enrolled at a grantee university Tutoring & advising Summer sessions

AAIP Pre-Admission Workshop

Brings AAIP member physicians together with AI/AN college students, public health professionals, and university admissions staff to give students the information and skills needed to get through health professions school admissions. Practical, direct, and run by people who have been where you are. AAIP also runs additional programs for college students and a National Youth Summit.

College students & applicants Admissions prep Direct physician mentorship

Indigenous Health Career Collaborative

A collected hub of resources for Indigenous students pursuing health careers — programs, funding, and guidance gathered in one place. Useful as a cross-check against this page, since new programs appear there regularly.

All levels Resource hub

Also worth a look

  • M-PATH — a pathway program listed in the Indigenius research. Check the program page directly for current eligibility and dates.
  • OSU Office of American Indians in Medicine and Science — runs four distinct American Indian pipeline programs, including Native Explorers above.

Organizations & conferences

Find your people early. Membership, conferences, and mentorship from these groups will carry you further than any single application. Several offer student rates and travel scholarships.

Association of American Indian Physicians (AAIP)

The central professional organization for Native physicians, and the connective tissue across much of this page. Three of the five leaders featured in Stories & Voices have served as its President. Students are welcome — join early.

American Indian Science and Engineering Society (AISES)

Large network for Native students in STEM, with chapters at many universities, a substantial national conference, and its own scholarships. Strong for students on research and laboratory paths, and a reliable place to meet graduate program recruiters.

SACNAS

Advances Chicano/Hispanic and Native American scientists from college through career. Their national conference is one of the best places for an undergraduate to present research for the first time.

National Tribal Health Conference (NIHB)

A week-long national gathering on tribal health, behavioral health, and public health, run by the National Indian Health Board. NIHB has offered scholarships for youth and elders from federally recognized tribes to attend, covering flights, hotel, registration, and a daily per diem. If you want to see what Native health policy actually looks like in practice, this is the room.

Students & youth welcome Travel scholarships offered

UNITY — United National Indian Tribal Youth

A national Native youth leadership organization behind the 25 Under 25 award, with regional gatherings, youth councils, and a yearly national conference. A good first national network for a high school student.

Johns Hopkins Center for American Indian Health

Research and programs grounded in tribal partnership, including the Native Vision youth program. A good place to look for internships and public health research experience.

Udall Foundation

Offers the Udall Undergraduate Scholarship for students committed to tribal public policy or Native health care, and a Native American Congressional Internship placing students in Washington, D.C. The health care track is explicitly for students pursuing careers serving Native communities.

After graduation

Worth knowing about now, because it changes how much debt you can reasonably take on.

IHS Loan Repayment Program

IHS repays a substantial share of qualifying student loans for health professionals who commit to a two-year service obligation at an Indian health program facility, with the option to extend until the balance is gone. For many graduates this is the difference between a manageable debt load and an unmanageable one.

After graduation 2-year service commitment Renewable until paid off

The advice every guest gave, in one line

Ask. Email the professor, the physician, the program director — the person who looks unreachable. Nearly everyone featured on this site got where they are because someone invested in them early, and almost all of them say the same thing: they were willing to ask, and the answer was yes more often than they expected.

Program details, award amounts, and eligibility rules change year to year. Everything here was checked against each program's own materials, but always confirm on the program's site before you apply. If you know of an opportunity that belongs on this page, tell us.
