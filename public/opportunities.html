Opportunities & Support

Money and mentorship exist for Indigenous students in the health professions — most students simply never hear about them. These are real, currently operating programs. Deadlines shift each year, so always confirm on the program's own site before you plan around a date.

Scholarships

Apply to several. These are not mutually exclusive, and students routinely stack them.

Indian Health Service Scholarship Program

The most directly relevant funding on this list, in three tiers. The Health Professions Preparatory Scholarship supports undergraduates taking prerequisite courses leading into a health professions degree. The Pre-Graduate Scholarship supports undergraduates working toward a bachelor's that leads to a health career. Neither carries a service commitment. The Health Professions Scholarship covers tuition and fees plus a monthly stipend for students already in a health professions program, in exchange for service at an IHS or tribal facility after graduation. Open to members and descendants of federally recognized, state-recognized, or terminated tribes. Awards are ranked on a 100-point system; minimum 2.0 cumulative GPA.

Undergraduate & professional Tribal enrollment or descent required No service commitment (Prep & Pre-Grad)

Cobell Scholarship

Funds Native American students in undergraduate, graduate, vocational, and fellowship study. Created from the Cobell settlement, and one of the largest sources of need- and merit-based aid available to Native students. Undergraduate and graduate applications typically open in the winter with a spring deadline; vocational applications run year-round.

All levels Vocational tracks too Confirm current deadline

Native Forward Scholars Fund

Formerly the American Indian Graduate Center. Administers many scholarship programs across undergraduate, graduate, and professional study, including awards specifically for health professions students. Their Scholarship Finder is the single best tool for discovering awards you did not know existed — start there before searching anywhere else.

Undergraduate & graduate Multiple awards

American Indian College Fund

Awards roughly 6,000 scholarships each year to American Indian and Alaska Native undergraduate and graduate students (including descendants). One application puts you in consideration for many awards. Applications generally open in spring for the following academic year.

~6,000 awards per year One application, many awards

BIA Higher Education Grant & tribal education programs

The least publicized and most overlooked money available. The Bureau of Indian Affairs funds higher education grants, and nearly every tribe runs its own higher education department with scholarships for its citizens. Call your tribe's education office directly — these awards are often less competitive than national ones simply because fewer people apply.

Often less competitive Contact your tribe directly

IHS Loan Repayment Program

Not a scholarship, but worth knowing about early because it changes the math on how much debt you can afford to take on. IHS repays a substantial share of qualifying student loans for health professionals who commit to a two-year service obligation at an Indian health program facility, with the option to extend until the balance is gone.

After graduation 2-year service commitment

Pathway & pipeline programs

Structured programs built specifically to get Native students into and through health professions school. Several are free.

Wy'east Medicine Pathway

A tuition-free, 10-month post-baccalaureate program for American Indian and Alaska Native learners preparing for medical school, running mid-September through early June. Culturally rooted specifically for AI/AN students, it prepares you for the academic content and the spaces you will be navigating in medical school. One of the strongest options for a student whose application needs strengthening.

Tuition-free Post-baccalaureate AI/AN specific

INMED — American Indians into Medicine

A federal grant program supporting universities to enroll and support AI/AN students in health careers. Programs provide tutoring, career counseling, help with scholarship and financial aid applications, summer educational sessions, and travel grants to health conferences. Current grantee institutions include the University of Alaska Anchorage, the University of New Mexico, the University of North Dakota, and the University of Wisconsin.

Tutoring & advising Summer sessions Four universities

Mayo Clinic Native American Pathway Program

A five-week virtual summer program offering coaching for medical school readiness, career exploration, an introduction to scientific research, and remote clinical observation. The curriculum is built on culturally resonant practices with attention to identity, resilience, and well-being. Co-founded by a leader featured in the broader Indigenius project.

Virtual 5 weeks, summer Research exposure

Summer Health Professions Education Program (SHPEP)

A free national summer program for college freshmen and sophomores interested in the health professions. Housing, meals, and a stipend are typically covered. If you are in your first two years of college and only do one thing from this page, apply to this — it is free, competitive in a good way, and opens doors at the schools that host it.

Free, with stipend College years 1–2 Applications close early in the year

AAIP Pre-Admission Workshop

Brings AAIP member physicians together with AI/AN college students, public health professionals, and university admissions staff to give students the information and skills needed to get through health professions school admissions. Practical, direct, and run by people who have been where you are. AAIP also runs additional programs for college students and a National Youth Summit.

Admissions prep Direct physician mentorship

Organizations to know

Find your people early. Membership, conferences, and mentorship from these groups will carry you further than any single application.

Association of American Indian Physicians (AAIP)

The central professional organization for Native physicians, and the connective tissue across much of this page. Three of the five leaders featured in Stories & Voices have served as its President. Students are welcome — join early.

American Indian Science and Engineering Society (AISES)

Large network for Native students in STEM, with chapters at many universities, a substantial national conference, and its own scholarships. Strong for students on research and laboratory paths.

SACNAS

Advances Chicano/Hispanic and Native American scientists from college through career. Their national conference is one of the best places for an undergraduate to present research for the first time and meet graduate program recruiters.

Johns Hopkins Center for American Indian Health

Research and programs grounded in tribal partnership, including the Native Vision youth program. A good place to look for internships and public health research experience.

Udall Foundation

Offers the Udall Undergraduate Scholarship for students committed to tribal public policy or Native health care, and a Native American Congressional Internship placing students in Washington, D.C. The health care track is explicitly for students pursuing careers serving Native communities.

The advice every guest gave, in one line

Ask. Email the professor, the physician, the program director — the person who looks unreachable. Nearly everyone featured on this site got where they are because someone invested in them early, and almost all of them say the same thing: they were willing to ask, and the answer was yes more often than they expected.

Indigenius

A platform for Indigenous student advancement in healthcare. Centering Indigenous voices, knowledge, and mentorship.
