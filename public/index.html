Indigenous pathways into healthcare

Our people belong in medicine.

Indigenius exists for every Native student who has wondered whether a career in healthcare is really for them. Here you will find the professions, the people who walk these paths, and the programs that will help you get there.

The gap we are closing

American Indian and Alaska Native people make up 2.9% of the United States population. We are nowhere near that share of the people who care for it.

0.7% of higher education enrollment is AI/AN
0.3% of attending physicians are AI/AN
0.4% of nurses identify as Native American
25% vacancy rate for health professionals on tribal lands

Figures drawn from the Indigenius capstone research. These numbers do not reflect a shortage of talent, passion, or dedication — they reflect a shortage of access.

Three disparities

Underfunded schools, staff shortages, historical trauma, and a lack of culturally competent guidance compound into three distinct barriers. Every part of this site is built to address one of them.

Access

183 Bureau of Indian Education schools across 64 reservations graduate students at a rate 13% below the national average, with chronic absenteeism at more than double the rate of white peers. Students arrive at the starting line a step behind — not for lack of ability, but for lack of resources.

Awareness

Healthcare is far bigger than becoming a doctor. Most students never learn that public health, research, nursing, therapy, social work, and dozens of other professions exist, or what it actually takes to enter them.

Aspiration

There are thousands of Native physicians, researchers, and public health leaders working right now. Our youth need to see them, learn from them, and know that becoming one of them is possible.

About this project

Indigenius began as a senior capstone project in the Department of Biology at Stanford University, built on a simple premise: to improve Indigenous health, we must first improve Indigenous representation in the people who deliver it.

Short-term contracted physicians are brought in to meet tribal health needs but often lack the cultural competence and long-term commitment that Native communities deserve. That deepens a distrust with deep historical roots, and the cycle continues — less care, more disease. Breaking it requires providers who come from these communities and intend to return to them.

This site gathers the information, the voices, and the opportunities in one place, so that no Indigenous student has to piece this path together alone.
