A Pathway Into Healthcare

Our people belong in medicine.

By bringing together health information, resources, and voices of American Indian and Alaska Native health care leaders, Indigenius expands opportunities for Native youth, strengthens Indigenous representation in health care, and inspires a healthier future across Indian Country.

The gap we are closing

American Indian and Alaska Native people make up 2.9% of the United States population, but lack representation in health care.

0.7% of higher education enrollment is AI/AN
0.3% of attending physicians are AI/AN
0.4% of nurses identify as Native American
25% vacancy rate for health professionals on tribal lands

Figures drawn from the Indigenius capstone research. These numbers do not reflect a shortage of talent, passion, or dedication — they reflect a shortage of access.

Three disparities

Underfunded schools, staff shortages, historical trauma, and a lack of culturally competent guidance compound into three distinct barriers.

Access

183 Bureau of Indian Education schools across 64 reservations graduate students at a rate 13% below the national average, with chronic absenteeism at more than double the rate of white peers. Students arrive at the starting line a step behind — not for lack of ability, but for lack of resources.

Awareness

Healthcare is far bigger than becoming a doctor, and no one heals a community alone. Nurses, social workers, epidemiologists, and many more all hold a seat on that team. Most students never learn these professions exist, or what it actually takes to enter them.

Aspiration

There are thousands of Native physicians, researchers, and public health leaders working right now. Our youth need to see them, learn from them, and know that becoming one of them is possible.

About this project

Indigenius began as a senior capstone project in the Department of Biology at Stanford University, built on a simple premise: to improve Indigenous health, we must first improve representation in the people who deliver it.
